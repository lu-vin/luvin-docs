The card shows what each route does. The **Success and routes** tab of the step panel is where you manage them: what counts as success, the routes in the order they are checked, and the rules the step inherits from the workflow. The rules themselves live in the panel of **Start**.

## Example: a specific route before a general one

`createOrder` has two failure routes: one for any error, and one for 409 that goes back to the product list. Arazzo takes the **first** route that applies, so the general one, listed first, always wins and the 409 route never runs.

<Steps>
1. Click the card of `createOrder`. Its panel opens over the canvas.
2. Click the tab **Success and routes · 2**.
3. Under **Routes**, find **If it fails with 409**. It is number 2.
4. Click **↑**. Its tooltip says **Check it earlier**.
</Steps>

![The Success and routes tab of createOrder: Routes, in order, with If it fails with 409, its sentence, its Arazzo line and the warning about the loop](/media/en/design-routes-panel.webp)

The 409 route is now number 1, on the card and in the file.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Before">

```yaml
onFailure:
  - name: stop
    type: end
  - name: on409
    type: goto
    stepId: listProducts
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 409
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="After">

```yaml
onFailure:
  - name: on409
    type: goto
    stepId: listProducts
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 409
  - name: stop
    type: end
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Open the tab

<Steps>
1. In the **Design** tab, click a card, or click **⤢** in its header (**Open in the panel**).
2. Click **Success and routes**. The number next to it is how many routes the step has.
</Steps>

The panel opens over the canvas, and the canvas moves just enough to keep the card in view. **Esc** or **×** closes it. Going to a route warning from [To review](/design/review/) opens this tab for you.

## It succeeds if

The first section, **It succeeds if** · **all are met**, lists the success criteria of the step, one per row, with their type: **simple**, **jsonpath**, **regex** or **xpath**.

- The first row is the status: `$statusCode ==` and a list with the 2xx statuses the contract documents. Choose the one you expect. A new step starts with the lowest one, or 200 if the contract documents none.
- The other rows are the criteria written in the YAML, for example `$response.body#/capture_rate >= 0` or a JSONPath. **Edit in the YAML** takes you there to change them. See [Check more than the status](/recipes/criteria/).

A messaging step has no status. Its row explains what counts as success for a message, with **Edit in the YAML**. See [Messaging steps](/design/messaging/).

If a criterion can never hold, a box **To review** appears above it, with **Change it** when Luvin can rewrite it. See [A JSONPath condition that never holds](/design/review/#a-jsonpath-condition-that-never-holds).

## Routes

The section **Routes** · **in order; the first that matches wins** lists the routes of the step, on success and on failure, in the order of the Arazzo. Each one has:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| A number | Its place in its list. |
| A title | **If it fails with 429**, **If it fails: other error**, **If it passes** or **If it passes and** followed by the condition. |
| What it does, in one sentence | *Retries up to 3 · every 2 s.* |
| Its Arazzo, in one line | `onFailure · type: retry · retryAfter: 2 · retryLimit: 3 · criteria: $statusCode == 429` |
| Buttons | **↑** (**Check it earlier**), **↓** (**Check it later**), **✎** (**Change the route**) and **✕** (**Remove the route**). |

Routes on success and routes on failure are separate lists in Arazzo: ↑ and ↓ move a route only within its own list.

**✎** opens the route editor with that route. **✕** removes it at once and shows **Route removed.** with **Undo**. See [Add or change a route](/design/branch-menu/).

Each route also has **▷ Probe**, which makes it run without calling the API, and a mark that says whether a run already took it. See [Probe routes and coverage](/test/probe-routes/).

When a route has a problem, such as a target step that no longer exists or a loop with no limit, a line **⚠** under it says what happens and what to do. See [To review](/design/review/).

### No routes yet

Without routes of its own, the section says *No routes of its own: if it passes, it goes on to the next step; if it fails, the Start rules apply and, if none applies, the workflow fails.*

### Statuses without a route

Below the routes, each status the contract declares and nothing handles has a row with **Ends**, **Retries** and **Choose what to do**. See [Declared statuses without a route](/design/branch-other-status/#declared-statuses-without-a-route-one-click).

### Add a route

At the end of the section, **＋ What to do if it fails** and **＋ Detour if it passes and a condition holds** open the route editor. A messaging step only has the second one.

### Reusable routes

A route can be a reusable of the file, defined once and used in several steps. It shows **♻** and its name, with **Detach**, which gives the step its own copy. A failure route for a single status that is not a reusable has **Save as reusable**. See [Reusables](/design/reusables/).

## From the workflow

When the workflow has rules, the tab ends with **From the workflow** · **for every step, after its own**. Each rule shows its status, what it does and its Arazzo line.

The step uses them only when none of its own routes applies. To handle that status differently in this step only, click **Change it for this step only**: Luvin copies the rule into the routes of the step, where you can change it.

## Rules for every step

Some responses mean the same in every call of an API: a 429 when you go over the rate limit, a 503 during maintenance. Instead of one route per step, give the workflow a rule.

<Steps>
1. Click **Start**, the green button at the top of the canvas. Its panel opens.
2. Find **If any step fails**.
3. Click **＋ Rule for every step**. The route editor opens, titled **Rule for every step**.
4. Type the status, choose what to do, and click **Add**.
</Steps>

![The Start panel: under If any step fails, the rule 401 → ends the workflow (End) as failed, and ＋ Rule for every step](/media/en/design-start-rules.webp)

The editor offers the same options as for a step, except **Go to another step**: a rule belongs to the whole workflow, not to one place in it.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="In the app">

**Start** now says **· 1 rule**. The panel lists `429 ↻ retries up to 3 · every 2 s`, with **↑**, **↓**, **✎** and **✕**. On every card, **On failure** ends with **If none of these applies, it uses the Start rules (429)**.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="In the YAML">

```yaml title="shop.arazzo.yaml" {3-9}
workflows:
  - workflowId: placeOrder
    failureActions:
      - name: retry429
        type: retry
        retryAfter: 2
        retryLimit: 3
        criteria:
          - condition: $statusCode == 429
    steps:
      # …
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

Rules on success (`successActions`) written in the YAML show in the same list, and you can reorder, change and remove them the same way. Without rules, the section says **No rules: each step decides on its own.**

The panel of **Start** also has the inputs of the workflow, the workflows it depends on and the reusables of the file. See [Workflows that use other workflows](/design/dependencies/) and [Reusables](/design/reusables/).
