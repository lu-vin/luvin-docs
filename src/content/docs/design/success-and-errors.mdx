Every step needs two decisions: which response means it went well, and what to do when the API answers something else. Luvin sets the first from the contract and leaves the second to you. Both are in the **Success and routes** tab of the step panel, and the routes are also on the card of the step.

This page is the overview. For how a run moves from step to step after each route, read [Routes: what happens after each step](/design/branches/).

## Example: a Pokémon that does not exist

In **Pokemon card**, the first step ends the workflow when the API answers 404, so "the Pokémon does not exist" is a known outcome and not a crash.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="In the app">

  In the panel of `getPokemon`, the **Success and routes** tab shows **It succeeds if** with `$statusCode ==` **200** and a JSONPath criterion. Under **Routes**, **If it fails with 404** says *Ends the workflow (End) as failed.* On the card, **On failure** has the row `404 → ends the workflow (End) as failed`, with a red dashed arrow to **End of workflow**.

  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="In the YAML">

```yaml title="pokemon.arazzo.yaml" {7-11}
- stepId: getPokemon
  successCriteria:
    - condition: $statusCode == 200
    - context: $response.body
      condition: $.abilities[?(@.is_hidden == false)]
      type: jsonpath
  onFailure:
    - name: pokemonNotFound
      type: end
      criteria:
        - condition: $statusCode == 404
```

  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## What counts as success

<Steps>
1. In the **Design** tab, click a step.
2. In its panel, click the tab **Success and routes**.
3. Under **It succeeds if**, open the list next to `$statusCode ==`. It has the 2xx statuses the contract documents for the operation.
4. Choose the status you expect.
</Steps>

A new step starts with the lowest documented 2xx, or 200 if the contract documents none. In the file it is written as `$statusCode == 200`. Any other response makes the step fail.

### More than the status

A step can also check the content of the response. These extra criteria are written in the [YAML tab](/design/arazzo-editor/). The tab lists them below the status, each with its type (**simple**, **jsonpath**, **regex** or **xpath**) and the link **Edit in the YAML**. The card shows them under **Success criteria**. All the criteria must hold at once.

In **Pokemon card**, the step `getSpecies` checks the status and a field:

```yaml title="pokemon.arazzo.yaml" {3}
successCriteria:
  - condition: $statusCode == 200
  - condition: $response.body#/capture_rate >= 0
```

![The Success and routes tab of getSpecies: It succeeds if with the status 200 and the criterion $response.body#/capture_rate >= 0, each with its type, and the link Edit in the YAML](/media/en/design-criteria-panel.webp)

A JSONPath criterion written as a comparison, such as `$.capture_rate >= 0`, never holds. [To review](/design/review/#a-jsonpath-condition-that-never-holds) warns about it and rewrites it for you. See [Check more than the status](/recipes/criteria/) for runnable examples.

## What happens on each error

Without a route, a step that fails stops the workflow with a generic failure. A route says what to do instead.

| Option in the route editor | What the workflow does |
|---|---|
| **Retry** | Calls the step again, with a wait and a limit. For 429, 500 or 503. |
| **Renew and retry** | Runs another workflow, such as the login, and calls the step again. For 401. |
| **Go to another step** | Jumps to another step of the workflow, earlier or later, and goes on from there. |
| **Continue in another workflow** | Hands control to another workflow. It does not come back. |
| **End the workflow** | Stops at this step, with a known reason. The workflow ends as failed. |

### Add a route for an error

<Steps>
1. On the card, click **＋ What to do if it fails**. Or, for a status the contract declares and nothing handles, click **Choose what to do** on its red row.
2. In **If it responds**, type the status or click it next to **the contract says:**.
3. Choose what to do and fill in what the option asks for.
4. Check **How it looks in Arazzo (onFailure)** and click **Add**.
</Steps>

For a 404 that ends the workflow, Luvin writes:

```yaml
onFailure:
  - name: on404
    type: end
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 404
```

The same buttons are in the **Success and routes** tab. There, a status without a route also has **Ends** and **Retries**, which add the route in one click.

Each option is explained in [Add or change a route](/design/branch-menu/). Statuses the contract does not declare, such as 429 or 503, are in [A route for any status](/design/branch-other-status/). For a status that can come from any step, add a rule once in **Start**: see [Rules for every step](/design/routes-panel/#rules-for-every-step).

Every route also gives you a test: Luvin generates a case that simulates that response and checks that the route fires. See [Cases](/test/cases/).

### Detour when the step passes

A step that passes can also take a route, when a value of its response holds. For example, if the Pokémon is legendary, go to another step. Click **＋ Detour if it passes and a condition holds** on the card, choose an output, type its value next to **is**, and choose **Go to another step**, **Continue in another workflow** or **End successfully**. See [Add or change a route](/design/branch-menu/#if-it-passes-a-condition-on-a-value).

### A new step for a route

Sometimes an error needs its own call, for example to list Pokémon when the species is not found. In the route editor, choose **Go to another step** and click **or a new step…**. The step you pick goes at the end of the workflow, the error jumps to it with a route `on404`, and the last step of the happy path gets a route on success named `done` that ends the run before the new step.

[A new step for a route](/design/branch-new-step/) shows the whole flow, the YAML before and after, and how to undo it. To add a step in another place of the workflow, use the **+** on the arrows between steps: see [Insert a step](/design/insert-step/).

Runnable examples: [Retry, jump and end on errors](/recipes/error-branches/) and [Expect an error](/recipes/expected-error/).

## Retries and slow calls

### What a retry does

When a step does not pass and its route is a retry, Luvin sends the step again. Each try appears in the results as, for example, **Retry 1 of 3 (action "retry429")**. If the last try fails too, the results say **The 3 retries were exhausted** and the step fails.

| Setting | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| How many times | `retryLimit`. The route editor proposes 3. If a route written in the YAML has none, Luvin retries once. |
| How long to wait | `retryAfter`, in seconds, between tries. The route editor proposes 2. |
| Real API limit | <Badge text="Max 10 s" variant="caution" /> Luvin never waits more than 10 seconds between two tries, even if `retryAfter` is larger. |
| Simulation | It does not wait at all. |
| Where the wait comes from | Only from `retryAfter`. Luvin does not read a `Retry-After` header of the response. |

### Run another step or workflow before the retry

A `retry` route can also name something to run first. Luvin runs it, then calls the failed step again. Use it to renew a token: the call answers 401, a login runs, the call is tried again.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Workflow: workflowId">

```yaml {4}
onFailure:
  - name: renew401
    type: retry
    workflowId: login
    retryAfter: 0
    retryLimit: 1
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 401
```

**Renew and retry**, in the route editor, writes this. Luvin runs the workflow, in this file or in another Arazzo file, and stores its outputs so the retried step can read them. On the card the row reads `401 ↻ runs «Login» and retries (up to 1)`.

  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Step: stepId">

```yaml title="retry-step.arazzo.yaml" {4}
onFailure:
  - name: loginAndRetry
    type: retry
    stepId: login
    retryLimit: 2
    criteria:
      - condition: $statusCode == 401
```

Luvin runs the step `login` of the same workflow first. The route editor does not write this form: write it in the [YAML tab](/design/arazzo-editor/). The card shows it as `401 ↻ runs step 2 and retries (up to 2)`. The step must exist: if not, the analysis says **Action "loginAndRetry" (retry) points to the non-existent step "login".**

  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

In the results, the try shows as **Retry 1 of 2: runs step "login" first (action "loginAndRetry")**. Luvin waits `retryAfter` before each retry.

Runnable example: [Log in again and retry](/recipes/retry-step/).

### Cut a slow call with `timeout`

A step can carry a `timeout`, in milliseconds. If the API does not answer in that time, Luvin cuts the call and the step fails with **The API did not answer within 3000 ms (step timeout).** The step panel has no field for it. Write it in the [YAML tab](/design/arazzo-editor/), inside the step:

```yaml
steps:
  - stepId: getOrder
    operationId: $sourceDescriptions.shop.getOrder
    timeout: 3000
```

See [Failure reasons](/reference/failure-reasons/) for how to read the message. For a step that waits for a message, `timeout` means something else: see [Messaging steps](/design/messaging/).

## See the routes on the canvas

Each card lists its routes in **If it passes and matches** and **On failure**, one row per route, in the order they are checked. Each route has an arrow on the left side of the cards, in the color of its scenario: green when it passes and a condition holds, red dashed when it fails, blue for a retry and amber when it continues in another workflow. The arrow goes to its real target: a step, the same card for a retry, or **End of workflow**.

![A card with its On failure section: the 409 row of createOrder goes to listProducts, and a red dashed arrow runs along the left of the cards up to that step](/media/en/design-jump-line.webp)

**View → Routes**, at the top right of the canvas, has the legend. Untick **Show the routes** to hide them when you want to read only the data. See [Routes: what happens after each step](/design/branches/#the-arrows).

## Check that every response is handled

Each status the contract declares and nothing handles is a red row on its card, `500 ✕ no route: the workflow fails`, and [To review](/design/review/) counts them as **without a route**.

The **Overview** tab of the workflow also has a section, **Response coverage**, that compares the contract with the workflow.

<Steps>
1. Open the **Overview** tab.
2. Open **Response coverage**. Its badge reads, for example, **5/7 handled**.
</Steps>

The table has one row per step and documented status:

| Handling in the Arazzo | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✓ **Success** | The status is the one the step expects. |
| ✓ **Action** | A route handles it. The name of the route follows. |
| ✕ **Unhandled** | Nothing handles it. If the API answers this, the workflow fails with a generic error. |

You do not have to handle everything. Handle the errors your application treats differently, such as "not found" or "not approved".

![Response coverage with 7/11 handled: one row per step and status, with Success, Action or Unhandled](/media/en/design-coverage.webp)
