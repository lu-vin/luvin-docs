The route editor writes every Arazzo action without YAML: retry, renew and retry, go to another step, continue in another workflow and end. It asks for the status or the condition, explains each option in one line and shows how the route looks in Arazzo while you fill it in.

## Example: retry a 503 of getPokemon

The PokéAPI can answer 503 when it is busy. Retry twice, waiting two seconds.

<Steps>
1. Open **Pokemon card** and click the **Design** tab.
2. On the card of `getPokemon`, at the end of **On failure**, click **＋ What to do if it fails**. The editor opens. Its title is the step number and title, followed by **what to do if it fails**.
3. In **If it responds**, type `503`.
4. Click **Retry**. The editor shows **Wait** `2` **s, up to** `3` **times**.
5. Change `3` to `2`.
6. Check the preview and click **Add**.
</Steps>

![The route editor of step 1 of Pokemon card: If it responds 503, Retry, Wait 2 s, up to 2 times, and the preview of the onFailure](/media/en/design-route-dialog.webp)

The card gets the row `503 ↻ retries up to 2 · every 2 s`, with a blue loop back into the card. A notice says **Route added.** with **Undo**.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="How it looks in Arazzo (onFailure)">

```yaml title="pokemon.arazzo.yaml"
- stepId: getPokemon
  # …
  onFailure:
    - name: retry503
      type: retry
      retryAfter: 2
      retryLimit: 2
      criteria:
        - condition: $statusCode == 503
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

![The card of getPokemon after adding the route: under On failure, the 404 that ends the workflow and the blue row 503 that retries up to 2 times](/media/en/design-route-card.webp)

## Open the editor

| From | What it opens |
|---|---|
| **＋ What to do if it fails**, at the end of **On failure** on the card | A route for when the step fails (`onFailure`). |
| **＋ Detour if it passes and a condition holds**, on the card | A route for when the step passes and a value of its response holds (`onSuccess`). |
| **Choose what to do**, on a red row `422 ✕ no route: the workflow fails` | A failure route with that status already filled in. |
| The pencil at the end of a route row | That route, to change it. Its tooltip says **Change the route**. |
| **Add route…** in the right-click menu of a card, or the **R** key with a step selected | A failure route for that step. |
| **＋ What to do if it fails** and **＋ Detour if it passes and a condition holds** in the **Success and routes** tab of the step panel | The same as on the card. |
| **＋ Rule for every step**, in the panel of **Start** | A rule of the workflow. See [Rules for every step](/design/routes-panel/#rules-for-every-step). |

## 1. Say when the route applies

### If it fails: a status

**If it responds** takes a three-digit status, for example `404`. Next to it, **the contract says:** lists the error statuses the contract declares for the operation. Click one to use it.

You can type any status, also one the contract does not declare. See [A route for any status](/design/branch-other-status/).

### If it passes: a condition on a value

**If the value** lists the outputs of the step. Choose one, and type what it must be next to **is**. It starts with `true`.

For example, with the output `isLegendary` of `getSpecies` and the value `true`, the route applies when the species is legendary. Luvin writes the condition on the response, `$response.body#/is_legendary == true`.

If the step has no outputs, the editor says **The step has no outputs: declare one to use it in the condition.** See [Outputs](/design/outputs/).

<Aside type="note">
The editor writes equality conditions (`==`). For `!=`, `>`, a `regex`, an `xpath` or several conditions at once, write the route in the [YAML tab](/design/arazzo-editor/). The card and the panel still show it, and you can reorder or remove it.
</Aside>

## 2. Choose what to do

Under **What to do**, each option says what it is for.

| Option | What it does | Offered |
|---|---|---|
| **Retry** | *Calls the same step again, with a wait and a limit. For 429, 500 or 503.* | On failure |
| **Renew and retry** | *Runs another workflow (the login, for example) and retries the step. For 401.* | On failure, when there is another workflow |
| **Go to another step** | *Jumps to a step of this workflow, forward or back.* | On a step, not in a rule |
| **Continue in another workflow** | *Hands control to another workflow, from this file or another. It does not come back.* | When there is another workflow |
| **End the workflow** | *Ends here (End). The workflow ends as failed.* | On failure |
| **End successfully** | *Ends here (End) even if steps remain.* | If it passes |

After you choose, the editor asks only for what that option needs. **change**, next to the option, goes back to the list.

### Retry

**Wait** `2` **s, up to** `3` **times**. Change both numbers as you need. Luvin waits at most 10 seconds between two tries against the real API, even if you write more, and does not wait in simulation.

### Renew and retry

- **First, run**: the workflow to run before the retry, from this file or from another Arazzo file of the collection. Each option shows its name and where it is.
- **Retry up to** `1` **times**.

It writes a `retry` with `workflowId` and `retryAfter: 0`. The outputs of that workflow are available to the step when it is called again. Runnable example: [Log in and use the token](/recipes/login-token/).

### Go to another step

**Go to step** lists the other steps of the workflow, with their number and title. A step above makes a loop: the row says **goes back to step 1 · …**, and [To review](/design/review/) warns if nothing on the response stops it.

On a failure route with a status, **or a new step…** adds a step only for that response. See [A new step for a route](/design/branch-new-step/).

### Continue in another workflow

**Continue in** lists the workflows of this file and of the other Arazzo files of the collection. The canvas draws an exit box **Continues in «…»** next to the end.

When you pick a workflow from another file, Luvin adds that file to the sources of this Arazzo, of type `arazzo`, and writes `workflowId: $sourceDescriptions.<source>.<workflow>`.

### End the workflow, End successfully

Nothing more to fill in. The note says where the arrow goes: **The arrow will go to End. The workflow ends here as failed.**, or, for a route on success, **The arrow will go to End. The workflow ends successfully even if steps remain.**

### Apply a reusable instead

On a failure route of a step, when the file has reusable error actions for that status, the list ends with **Or apply a reusable:** and one button per reusable, marked ♻. Clicking one applies it to the step and closes the editor. See [Reusables](/design/reusables/).

## 3. Check it and add it

**How it looks in Arazzo (onFailure)**, or `onSuccess`, `failureActions` or `successActions`, shows the route as it will be written, with its name. While something is missing, it says **The condition or the target is missing.** and **Add** stays off.

<Steps>
1. Read the preview.
2. Click **Add** for a new route, or **Save** when you changed one.
</Steps>

A notice says **Route added.** or **Route changed.**, with **Undo**. You can also undo with ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z).

### Names

Luvin names each new route from what it does: `on404` (go to a step or end), `retry503`, `renew401`, or `if` plus the field for a condition on success, such as `ifIs_legendary`. If the name is taken in the same list, it adds `_2`, `_3`… When you change a route, it keeps its name, also a name you wrote yourself.

### One route per condition

Arazzo takes the first route whose conditions hold. A second route with the same condition would never run, so when you add a route for a status that already has one, Luvin replaces that route in its place instead of adding another.

## Change or remove a route

<Steps>
1. Click the pencil at the end of the row, on the card or in the **Success and routes** tab of the step panel.
2. Change the status, the condition, the option or its fields. The preview updates.
3. Click **Save**, or **Remove the route** to delete it.
</Steps>

**Remove the route** shows **Route removed.** with **Undo**. To change the order in which routes are checked, use the ↑ ↓ arrows of the Routes panel: see [The Routes panel and rules for every step](/design/routes-panel/).

To close the editor without changes, click **Cancel**.
