In the detail of a case, every step of the workflow is a card. Closed, it tells you how the step ended. Open, it shows the request that was sent, the response that came back and why the step passed or failed.

## Example: a criterion that was not met

In a shop workflow, `getProduct` checks that the product it gets back is the one it asked for: `$response.body#/sku == $inputs.sku`. Asked for `SKU-999`, the shop answers 200 with `"sku": "unknown"`. The step fails and explains what it expected and what it received:

```text
Criterion not met
sku
Expected   "SKU-999"
Received   "unknown"
```

![A failed step: Criterion not met, sku expected SKU-999 and received unknown, and the Criteria tab with the condition Not met](/media/en/test-verdict-inconclusive.webp)

Press **See in the response** to open the **Response** tab with that field highlighted in the body.

![The Response tab of the same step: the field sku is marked in red](/media/en/test-step-response.webp)

## The step card

From left to right, a card shows the step number, its status, its name, the method and path, and the HTTP status with the time and size of the response.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Succeeded** | The step met all its criteria. |
| **Succeeded with warnings** | The criteria were met, with something to review: the response does not match the contract, an output that later steps use was not found, or a messaging step was simulated. |
| **Failed** | The step did not meet its criteria, or there was no response. |
| **Not run** | The workflow stopped or ended before this step. |

Two more badges can appear: **Simulated**, and **Attempt 2** when the step was retried. A messaging step shows **Sent**, **Message received** or **No message** in place of the HTTP status.

Click a card to open or close it. Failed steps and steps with warnings open on their own.

Under the header, notes say why the step failed, which action fired (for example *↪ End of workflow (action "pokemonNotFound")*) and what you chose from a list.

## The tabs inside a step

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Criteria** | Each success criterion in plain words, with the technical expression below, whether it was **Met** or **Not met**, and the detail. |
| **Request** | The method, URL, headers and body that were sent, under **Line and headers** and **Body**. **Copy as cURL** copies the request for your terminal. |
| **Response** | The body, with a search box, and **Status and headers**. For a messaging step the tab is **Message**. |
| **Contract** | **The response matches the contract**, or the list of differences. |
| **Outputs** | The values the step exposes to the next steps. **Not in the response** marks one that was not found. |

![The Request tab of a step: Copy as cURL, the line and headers that were sent, and the empty body of a GET](/media/en/test-step-request.webp)

## Read the response of a step

Open the **Response** tab of a step. Under the headline, from top to bottom:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| A yellow box, **1 contract warning(s)** | Appears when the response differs from the contract. It shows the difference, for example the field that has the wrong type. |
| ‹ and › | **Previous warning** and **Next warning**. Move through the warnings, with a counter such as `1/3`. Both are off when there is only one. The body scrolls to the field of each warning. |
| **Search the body…** | Type a text to find it in the body. Next to the box, Luvin says how many lines match, for example **2 line(s)**. |
| The body | The JSON, colored. Fields that made a criterion fail are marked in red, and the fields that differ from the contract in yellow. You can fold objects and lists. |
| **Status and headers** | A folded list. Click it to read the headers of the response in a table. The number is how many there are. |

**Example.** Imagine `getPokemon` answers `id` as text when the contract says a number. The **Contract** tab lists the difference, and **Show in the body** opens the **Response** tab with that warning selected. Type `species` in the search box to find every line that has that word.

## Failures in plain words

A failed step names the kind of problem and explains it:

| Label | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Criterion not met** | A value was not the expected one. The note shows the field, the **Expected** value and the **Received** value. |
| **Error returned by the API** | The API answered an error. The note includes its status and message. |
| **Authentication** | The API answered 401 or 403. Check the credential. |
| **Data not available** | A value from an input or an earlier step was missing, so the request was sent incomplete. |
| **Network** | The API did not respond, or did not respond in time. See [Connection help](/dev-tools/connection-help/). |
| **Operation not found** | The operation of the step is not in the loaded contracts. |

## Contract warnings

Luvin compares every response with the contract: a status that is not documented, a required property that is missing, a wrong type, a value outside the allowed ones, a property the contract does not define.

A difference does not fail the step. It ends as **Succeeded with warnings**, with the label **Response out of contract**. In the **Contract** tab, **Show in the body** jumps to the field. In the **Response** tab, the ‹ and › buttons move from one warning to the next.

## A step that answers the expected error

Some cases expect an error: a rejection case, a response case, or a custom data case that expects a 404. The step that answers that error is what the case tests, so it is shown as passed:

- A badge, **Responded 404 (expected)**.
- A note, **This is what the case tests**, with what the case did on purpose and, after **The API said:**, the message of the API.
- The action that the error triggered.
- On the steps after it: *Not run: the workflow stopped at "…", which answered with the error this case expected.*

![A step that answered the expected error: Responded 404 (expected), This is what the case tests, The API said: Not Found, and the action that ended the workflow](/media/en/test-response-real-result.webp)

In these cases the **Criteria** tab shows a single criterion, for example **Responds 404 (what this case expects)**. The step's own success criteria are folded under *The step’s normal criteria (2): they do not apply in this case*.

If the step answers something else, the note says **Did not respond what the case expects**, for example *this case goes without credentials and expects 401, 403, but «createOrder» responded 201.*

![A step that did not respond what the case expects: createOrder answered 201 to a request without credentials](/media/en/test-verdict-failed.webp)

## Chosen at run time

When the run paused for you to [choose an item from a list](/test/lists/), the step that returned the list has a note, **Chosen at run time**, with the item and the value each parameter received. **From the list** means the list had a single item and it was used without asking.

## Secrets are masked

- The secrets and credentials of the environment appear as `••••` in requests, responses and outputs.
- Headers such as `Authorization`, tokens, API keys, passwords and cookies show only their first and last characters: `Bearer ey••••9Q`.
- **Copy as cURL** writes secrets as `<secret>`, so you can share it. **Copy with credentials** includes the real values: do not share it.

See [Privacy](/reference/privacy/).
